Part One — Until 1940

The roots of the Kleiner family are in Sokyriany — Secureni, Сокиряни — a town in northern Bessarabia, near the Dniester River. Over the years, the region passed between different rulers, and the local language and culture were influenced by Romanian, Russian, Ukrainian and Yiddish. The Kleiner family lived there before Shika was born, and there is evidence of other members of the Kleiner family in the town.

[[תמונה: Ukrain_Sokiryani.jpg | Sokyriany on the map of Ukraine]]

At the beginning of the twentieth century, three brothers from the Kleiner family lived in Sokyriany — Haim, Meir and Shmuel.

Haim Kleiner was born in 1872. His wife Perl was born in 1876. Their three children were Ovsey, born in 1915, Sheindle, born in 1917, and Yosef, born in 1921. Ovsey was the eldest, and the family called him Shika. His full name in Russian was Клейнер Овсей Хаймович — Ovsey, son of Haim Kleiner.

[[תמונה: Shika_Meir_Sam.jpeg | Shika and the two friends]]

Living near Haim's family were Shmuel, the youngest of the brothers, and the family of his brother Meir Kleiner. Shmuel, Meir and his family later left the region and emigrated to the United States, while Haim's family remained in Sokyriany.

[[תמונה: Kleiners.jpg | The only record of the family currently known to exist]]

The Beznos family also lived in the same town. The father, Yitzhak, son of Gersh, was born in 1887, and his wife Haika, Haica, in 1892. Their children were born one after another: Riva in 1915, Etya in 1917, Haim in 1919, Yankli in 1923, followed by several more brothers and sisters. Yitzhak, the father of the family, died in 1940.

Etya was the second daughter in the Beznos family. In the records, her name appears in a form close to Eidlea or Eidlia, and the family knew her as Etya or Etke. She grew up in Sokyriany almost alongside Shika Kleiner: he was born in 1915 and she in 1917. The two families, Kleiner and Beznos, were therefore part of the same relatively small Jewish community, and their younger generations grew up in the same town during the same years.

When Shika was born in 1915, Sokyriany was still part of the Russian Empire. After the First World War, the border changed, and Bessarabia became part of Romania. Shika therefore grew up in a world that began as Russian and became Romanian, and as an adult he was a Romanian citizen.

In 1937, Shika was drafted into the Romanian army. For about three years, he served as a soldier in Romania's army, far from family life in Sokyriany.

[[תמונה: Shika_solder.jpeg | Shika solder in Romaninan army]]

During those years, his relationship with Etya Beznos had already become a family of his own. Etya became Shika's wife. In this way, the two families from Sokyriany — Kleiner and Beznos — were joined into one family.

In 1940, Shika completed his service in the Romanian army and returned home to Sokyriany. He returned to Etya and to the two families living in the town: his parents Haim and Perl, his younger sister Sheindle and brother Yosef, and the Beznos family.

Around that same year, Shika and Etya's first daughter was born. They named her Nyusya — Нюся.

Thus, in 1940, Shika was twenty-five. He already had three years of service in the Romanian army behind him, and he returned to his hometown as a married man and a young father. Etya and their little daughter Nyusya waited for him at home, and members of both families from which they came still lived around them.

But in that very year, the world around them changed again.

In June 1940, Romania was forced to cede Bessarabia to the Soviet Union. The Soviet army entered the region, and Sokyriany, until then a Romanian town, became part of the Soviet Union. Within a short time, Shika went from being a former soldier in the Romanian army to having his home and family under Soviet rule.

Less than a year later, in the summer of 1941, with the outbreak of war between Germany and the Soviet Union, Shika was drafted again — this time into the Red Army.

Left behind in Sokyriany were Etya and little Nyusya, his parents Haim and Perl, his sister Sheindle, his brother Yosef and the Beznos family.

This began the next chapter in Shika's life: service in the Red Army, retreat eastward, combat engineering and the long journey that would eventually take him from the Caucasus back west as far as Budapest.
