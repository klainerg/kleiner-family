Part Two — The War

In 1941, Shika's life changed once again. A year earlier, he had returned to Sokyriany after about three years of service in the Romanian army. In June 1940, the region came under Soviet control, and in 1941, as the war spread to the Soviet Union, Shika was drafted into the Red Army.

Shika served in a combat engineering unit. He followed a very long path through the war: first retreating eastward with the Soviet army as far as the Caucasus region, and later, after the tide turned on the front, advancing westward again through the Soviet Union and Eastern Europe.

At the same time, a great tragedy befell the family that had remained in Sokyriany.

In the summer of 1941, the region returned to Romanian-German control. The Jews of Sokyriany and the surrounding area were rounded up, deported and taken on harsh journeys toward Transnistria. According to the memories passed down in the family, Shika's parents, Haim and Perl, his sister Sheindle and his brother Yosef did not reach their destination. All were killed or died during the deportation and the march.

[[קישור: deportation-route | Read about the deportation route and the ghetto]]

Shika himself escaped their fate because he was already a soldier in the Red Army at that time.

The war was also a personal tragedy for the young family Shika had established before it began. According to family memories, Shika and Anya were married before the war, and around 1940 their daughter Nyusya — Нюся — was born.

While Shika was at the front, Anya and Nyusya remained behind and were caught up in persecution and deportations. The family clearly preserved the memory that Nyusya died in a ghetto during the Holocaust.

Thus, while Shika travelled thousands of kilometres with the army at the front, the family world he had left almost disappeared. His parents and siblings were murdered or died during the deportation, and his first daughter, Nyusya, died in the ghetto.

According to family memories, his wartime journey reached Budapest. The battles for Budapest in the winter of 1944–1945 were among the hardest in the final phase of the Eastern Front. Reaching it meant that Shika had effectively travelled most of the Red Army's long path: from the great retreat eastward to the advance deep into Central Europe.

Shika received two medals. During the war, he used the certificates awarding those medals as paper for rolling cigarettes.

When the war ended for him, Shika was a different person from the one who had left Sokyriany. He had survived years of military service, retreat and prolonged war, but had lost almost all his close family and his first daughter.

After the war, he had to begin again.

Shika and Anya built their lives in Chernivtsi. Their children were born there, and the family from which the next generation of the Kleiner family grew became established: Pola, followed by the twins Fima and Itzik.

Sokyriany remained behind as his birthplace and the place where the Kleiner family had lived before the war. Chernivtsi became his home afterward.

Decades later, in 1985, Shika was awarded a Soviet decoration marking forty years since the victory.

[[תמונה: Medal_1985.jpeg | Decoration marking the 40th anniversary of the victory]]

In this sense, Shika's life connected two worlds. He was born in Sokyriany at a time when the region repeatedly changed rulers and borders; served first in the Romanian army and then in the Red Army; lost his family in the Holocaust; passed through the war from one front to another; and finally returned to civilian life and built a new family with Anya in Chernivtsi.

This was the family that carried on.
